Chapter X – The Margin of Memory

The road toward Archivon was quiet for days, until one rainy evening Pip decided to conduct a “small and completely controlled experiment.” With his Tanglewood Tripod, a bowl of water, Luna’s lantern, an alchemy die, and his mushroom companion gathered around the fire, he placed three strange parchments nearby: BURNED, DROWNED, and HIDDEN. He wondered aloud how Archivon could have been destroyed three different ways, and where its truth had gone if everyone remembered a different part of the story.

Then the lantern flame bent downward. The water rose. The mushroom squeaked. Ink spread across the parchment and swallowed Pip whole. Luna caught the tip of his tail, but the world folded away, and he vanished.

Luna awoke again and again at the same campsite, but each time something was wrong. Rain hung frozen in the air, the fire was cold, the forest unfinished, and Pip kept repeating the same question before disappearing. Eventually Luna discovered an unfamiliar brass device among Pip’s things. Touching it carried her into a memory of a rainy city square, where Guild officers marched through protesters and a masked woman wielded strange brass machinery. Her name was Lux, and she revealed that Luna did not belong in the memory. The device was her Wayfinder, and Lux had been trapped in a place called the Margin after discovering a machine built by the Cult to erase memories and people from the world.

The Margin was a realm made from forgotten moments and broken memories. Lux explained that objects which did not belong could connect one memory to another, and together she and Luna followed the trail until they found Lex, a translucent figure hiding among repeating memories. He had been trapped there as well, sending warnings and directions through Luna’s lantern. With Lux, Luna, and Lex reunited, the mystery of Pip’s disappearance became clearer: the Warden, a creature born from forgetting, had taken him.

The Warden had consumed forgotten memories for centuries, but now it wanted something new. Pip’s endless questions were dangerous because they could create something the Margin could not understand. The Warden believed Pip carried the source of the First Thought, and if it could tear him apart memory by memory, it could learn to create memories of things that had never happened. Truth itself would become impossible to separate from invention.

Following a trail of strange mushrooms, the companions passed through the Echo Maze, where false versions of Pip’s laughter tried to lead them astray. Eventually they reached an unfinished forest and fought living black scribbles called Redactions, creatures capable of erasing things from ever having existed. After the battle, Lux found a brass lighter that belonged to her. When she lit it, a forgotten memory of her childhood returned, and three candles burst into flame. The path ahead led to the Warden’s palace, a strange structure assembled from forgotten places and pieces of memories that no longer belonged anywhere.

Inside, the companions solved the palace’s shifting doors and descended into its heart. There they found Pip suspended in black strands while the Warden unfolded around him, filled with stolen faces and forgotten voices. The creature offered Lux a chance to erase her guilt and grief if she abandoned Pip, but she refused. The battle that followed tore the Warden apart, freeing Pip and scattering the stolen memories. As the palace collapsed, the companions discovered that Lux’s childhood memory contained the key to escape: light would always show the way home.

Using the three candles, they performed the ritual and opened a doorway back to their campsite. But Lex could not return. He was already a memory, and the Margin had become his home. He gave his candle to Pip and promised that he would remain within Luna’s lantern, listening, remembering, and offering advice that everyone would probably ignore. Luna, Lux, and Pip stepped through the flames and awoke beside their campfire, almost exactly where they had begun.

Their journey to Archivon continued, but the companions now understood that some things were not lost because they had been destroyed. Some were lost because someone had chosen to forget them. And somewhere within the lantern, an old friend was still watching, waiting, and remembering.
